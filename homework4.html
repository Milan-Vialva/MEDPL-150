Trinidad and Tobago Independence Day

Montage Work

Explanation

I wanted to explore the concept of Independence Day in my country, Trinidad and Tobago. It’s the celebration of the Trinidad and Tobago flag being raised for the first time on August 31st, 1962, acknowledging the new independent nation. It’s usually commemorated as a military-style parade, live soca, pan, and fireworks at the end of the day, which brings so many people together to be a part of an important time in the country’s history. Through my Photomonatage, I wanted to share the joy and importance of the Trinidad and Tobago flag, which is why there is a lot of the flag’s colours incorporated in my collage and blending of images.

I used images from Wiki Commons and other online sources that had articles about Trinidad and Tobago's independence. The images I chose reminded me of actual moments I saw with my own eyes when going to Independence Day parades and watching the fireworks from outside my house with my family, but they also give an abstract feeling of what it’s like celebrating Independence Day in Trinidad. When it came to putting the images together, it was a tedious process figuring out which images worked well together, which ones went above or below each other, and if I liked the composition differences, so I played a lot with sizing as well. I used the masking tool on most of the images, either to remove the background to give an overlay effect, like with the fireworks and outline of people cheering on the flag, or to manually remove the background, like with the soldiers. The blending tools were helpful when adding the image of a flag over the steel pan and changing the blend mode to soft light, which kept the texture of the pan but changed it to the colours of the flag. For the flag at the top, I edited the hue and saturation to kind of match the red colour as close as I can to the other shades of red in the images without overediting it.
