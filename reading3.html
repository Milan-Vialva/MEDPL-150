Reading and Writing Response #3

The article “The Graphic Novel: Motherhood in crisis” is a very unique way of informing others of a story. Usually, articles need to be in typed format and very long; in this article, the stories are told in a comic book format. As discussed in the lecture on user experience and interaction design, I noticed the article's interactive elements, such as moving to the next page or going back to a previous one, and its ability to pause. This format I found very unique and interesting. It encourages far more engagement than a regular text format since the reader has to pay attention and can manually control their pace. In terms of the illustrations, it pushes the story and actually tells it rather than having the reader just skim through words to understand. It gives you a visual of what you’re reading instead of leaving it up to you to interpret and imagine. This makes for an even deeper understanding of the narratives being told. Instead of making it an article that we’re used to and expect, like what Jakob’s Law suggests when it comes to websites, it found a way to make it genuinely occupy your attention and want to continue reading. The artwork makes it feel like an interactive animated show because of how it expresses movement, emotion, and each scene is portrayed. I found myself feeling empathetic because of how well the images combined with the words I was reading, taking them in at the same time. Another interface choice that holds the attention of the reader is the appearance of text a few seconds into the page you’re on. In chapter 3, ‘Kadiatu’s Story: The devil at my door’, one of the slides had the names of her children who have died show up one after the other, and sadly kept going until the 7th baby appeared. Kadiatu was drawn kneeling in front of what seemed like graves, as the text appeared about when they were born and how old they were. This choice emphasizes the heartache she went through with many of her children passing away because of sickness and poor healthcare systems. The fade-in animation of one line and the few-second interval of the next line, I think, also plays a part in the emotional aspect of the scene we’re reading and looking at. The mixture of the white clouds with the grayish overlay makes it feel sorrowful. Another example of the illustrations evoking a certain demeanor is in ‘Tettah’s Story: My punishment’ when she mentioned meeting her husband. The first slide that showed an image of her husband up close was of him smiling and had highlights on his face. The following slide, his features changed; they were harsh, his expression was upset, and the background went from maroon to a red-orange colour depicting his anger. This choice evokes a sense of danger or unease because of the drastic expression change paired with the text saying “I was happy to marry him” to “But things soon changed”. The addition of the real person being interviewed at the end brings the reader back to the fact that it’s not a sensitive comic but about a real person’s life and story.
